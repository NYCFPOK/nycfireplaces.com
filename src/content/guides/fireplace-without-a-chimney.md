In most homes, yes. Several kinds of fireplace are designed for exactly this.

## Direct-vent gas

A direct-vent gas fireplace is sealed behind glass. It draws air in and sends exhaust out through its own double-wall pipe, which runs to an exterior wall or the roof. No chimney is needed. This is what most new-construction and renovation fireplaces are.

What it needs: a gas supply and a workable route for the vent.

## Electric

An electric fireplace needs only power. It can be recessed into a wall, mounted on one, or built into a media unit. It is chosen mainly for the look, with optional heat.

## Water vapor

A water-vapor fireplace uses mist and light to create a flame effect. Nothing burns, so there is nothing to vent. It is a visual feature and does not heat the room.

## Ethanol

An ethanol fireplace burns liquid bioethanol and produces a real flame without a vent or gas line. It is used mainly for atmosphere.

## Apartments, condos and co-ops

Venting through an exterior wall is sometimes possible and sometimes not. Electric is the simplest option, and water vapor and ethanol are alternatives. Buildings often have their own rules, so speak to your board or management early. We can help you gather the product information they ask for.
