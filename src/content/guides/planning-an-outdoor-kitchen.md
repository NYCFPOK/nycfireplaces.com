Outdoor kitchens go more smoothly when decisions are made in the right order. This is the order we suggest.

## 1. Where it goes

Close enough to the house to be convenient, far enough that smoke is not a problem. Note whether it will be open to the sky or under a roof or pergola, since covered kitchens need thought about ventilation and clearances.

## 2. Utilities

Find out where gas, water and electricity can come from. Most grills are available for natural gas or propane. A sink needs water and drainage. Refrigeration, lighting and some grill features need power.

## 3. The structure

- **Custom masonry**, built on site and finished in stone or stucco.
- **Weatherproof cabinetry** in stainless or aluminum, in a powder-coated color.
- **A pre-engineered island**, delivered ready to finish.

## 4. The appliances

Start with the grill, because it sets the cut-out. Then decide what else earns a place: a side or power burner, refrigerator, ice maker, storage, a pizza oven, a bar.

Choose equipment **before** the island is built. Cut-out sizes are specific to each appliance, and a small difference can cause a big problem in finished stone.

## 5. Who does what

We can design, supply and install qualifying projects. If your own contractor or mason is building, we supply the equipment and the specifications they need. Either way, the scope is written down so everyone knows who is responsible for what.

## What to bring to a first conversation

A photo of the area, rough dimensions, and a sense of how you like to cook and entertain. That is enough to start a layout. We prepare 3D layouts to go along with outdoor kitchen purchases; ask what is included for your project.
