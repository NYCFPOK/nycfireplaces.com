People often ask for a "gas insert" when they mean something else, because the terms overlap. Here is the simple version.

## If you already have a wood-burning fireplace

You have two main routes.

**A gas insert** is a sealed firebox with a glass front. It slides into the existing opening and vents up the existing chimney. Choose it when you want real, controllable heat.

**Gas logs or a glass burner** sit inside your open firebox and connect to a gas line. You keep the open-hearth look and get a fire that lights instantly. Choose it when the look of the fire matters more than heating the room. Glass doors are optional and made to measure.

## If there is no fireplace there today

You are looking at a **new fireplace**, not an insert. The most common choice is a direct-vent gas fireplace. It is a sealed unit that vents through an exterior wall or the roof with its own pipe, so it does not need a chimney. Electric, water-vapor and ethanol fireplaces are options where gas or venting is not practical.

## A quick way to tell

- Existing fireplace, want heat: look at an insert.
- Existing fireplace, want the look: look at gas logs.
- No fireplace: look at a new direct-vent or electric fireplace.

## What we will ask

For an existing fireplace: the width, height and depth of the opening, whether it is masonry or a metal factory-built box, and whether gas is nearby. A few phone photos answer most of it.
